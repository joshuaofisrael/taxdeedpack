Florida tax deed surplus funds: who can claim excess proceeds

By the Tax Deed Pack research team · Updated November 5, 2026

When a Florida tax deed auction clears above the amounts owed for taxes and costs, the leftover money is called surplus or excess proceeds. Florida Statutes 197.582 controls how the clerk disburses that money. Former owners, lienholders, and bidders often talk past each other about who gets what.

What surplus means in plain English

The high bidder pays the winning bid (plus stamps and recording) to the clerk. From that pot, Florida Statutes 197.582 directs the clerk to pay statutory priorities: tax certificates, omitted taxes, and other listed amounts. If money remains after those payments, that remainder is surplus available to people entitled under the statute and the county’s claim process.1

Surplus is not a rebate to the bidder. It is also not automatic cash mailed the next day without paperwork. Clerks publish claim instructions because identity, heirship, and competing claims have to be sorted.

Why the disbursement ladder matters to researchers

Before you bid, the same statute helps you understand the clerk file. Municipal liens and community development district liens that survive under 197.552 can interact with how proceeds are applied.2 A parcel that looks “cheap” on the auction screen may still carry surviving government liens that never show up as bidder surplus.

If you are a former owner researching after the sale, start with the county clerk’s tax deed or surplus funds page, the case or certificate number, and any notice you received under the tax deed application process. Addresses used for notice often come from the tax deed application materials described around 197.502 and related notice sections.3

Practical checklist for surplus questions

  1. Confirm the sale actually closed and the deed issued (parcels can be canceled or pulled).
  2. Ask the clerk whether a surplus balance exists for that certificate or case number.
  3. Read the county’s claim form requirements (ID, probate papers, releases).
  4. Do not confuse surplus claims with redemption. Redemption closes earlier under 197.472.4
  5. If you are bidding on future sales, still research surviving liens; surplus math does not erase them.

FAQ

What are surplus funds after a Florida tax deed sale?

Surplus is money left after the clerk pays the amounts required by Florida Statutes 197.582 from the tax deed sale price. Eligible parties may claim it through the clerk’s process.1

Does the high bidder keep the surplus?

No. The bidder pays to acquire the tax deed interest. Surplus is disbursed under 197.582 to the parties the statute prioritizes.

How does a former owner learn surplus exists?

Through clerk notice practices and by checking the clerk’s tax deed or surplus funds page for that county. Procedures vary by clerk.

Is claiming surplus the same as getting the property back?

No. Surplus is about excess proceeds. It does not reverse the deed. Redemption ends earlier under 197.472.4

Should bidders care about surplus math?

Understanding 197.582 helps you read the clerk file and surviving lien risk. It is not a substitute for parcel due diligence.
